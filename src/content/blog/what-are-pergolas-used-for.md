Pergolas are used to make part of a garden more comfortable, useful and visually defined. They can create shade over a patio, shelter an outdoor dining area, support climbing plants, provide privacy or turn an unused corner into a focal point.

The important distinction is that not every pergola does every job. A traditional open-roof timber structure is ideal for plants and dappled shade, while a fixed or louvred roof is better when you want dependable cover from British rain.

## Eight common uses for a pergola

| Use | Most suitable setup |
|---|---|
| Shading a sunny patio | Slatted, retractable or louvred roof |
| Sheltering outdoor dining | Fixed or closing louvred roof |
| Creating an outdoor living area | Attached pergola with lighting and optional screens |
| Supporting climbing plants | Open timber frame |
| Adding privacy | Side screens, slatted panels or planting |
| Defining a garden zone | Freestanding pergola |
| Covering a hot tub or outdoor kitchen | Weather-resistant roof with suitable ventilation |
| Creating a garden feature | Timber or aluminium design that suits the house |

### 1. Providing shade

One of the main purposes of a pergola is to make a sunny patio or seating area more comfortable. Overhead beams break up direct sunlight, while a fabric canopy, dense planting or adjustable louvres can provide more complete shade.

Open slats only create partial shade, and the shaded area moves as the sun crosses the sky. If reducing strong midday sun is your priority, consider the direction the roof beams run and whether you need a canopy or adjustable roof rather than relying on an open frame.

### 2. Sheltering an outdoor dining area

A pergola can give a dining table a permanent home instead of leaving it exposed in the middle of a patio. The structure creates a sense of enclosure, and a covered roof can keep a passing shower from ending a meal.

For reliable rain cover, choose a fixed solid roof or a properly drained louvred system. A traditional open pergola may soften sunlight, but it will not keep furniture or diners dry.

### 3. Creating an outdoor living room

When positioned beside the house, a pergola can make the transition from kitchen or living room to garden feel more natural. Seating, lighting and heating can turn it into an outdoor lounge for reading, entertaining or spending time with family.

Side screens or sliding panels add protection from wind and low sun, although they also increase cost and make ventilation more important. Our guide to a [pergola with sides](/pergola-with-sides/) explains the main privacy and weather-protection options.

### 4. Supporting climbing plants

Traditional pergolas were designed in part to carry climbing plants. Wisteria, roses, grapevines and other climbers can soften the frame, add seasonal colour and gradually create natural shade.

Choose plants for the conditions in the exact position, not just their appearance. Some vigorous climbers become very heavy when mature, so the frame needs enough strength and the plants need regular pruning. Keep growth away from gutters, motors and moving roof parts.

### 5. Adding privacy

A pergola does not automatically block views because its sides are normally open. However, it provides a framework for timber slats, retractable screens, trellis or planting that can make a seating area feel less overlooked.

Privacy works best when you identify the actual sightline first. A single screened side may be enough to block a neighbouring window without closing in the whole structure or reducing light unnecessarily.

### 6. Dividing a garden into zones

A freestanding pergola can separate dining, lounging or cooking space from the rest of the garden without building solid walls. It is particularly useful in a large or open plot where furniture alone can look disconnected.

It can also turn an overlooked end of the garden into a destination. Explore the placement and design choices for [freestanding garden pergolas](/garden-pergolas/) if the structure will sit away from the house.

### 7. Covering a hot tub, barbecue or outdoor kitchen

Pergolas are often placed over features that benefit from a defined, partially protected area. Hot tubs gain privacy, while an outdoor kitchen becomes easier to organise with lighting, power and storage planned around a permanent structure.

Heat and ventilation need careful thought around barbecues, pizza ovens, fire pits and heaters. Follow the appliance manufacturer's clearance instructions, avoid trapping smoke beneath a roof and ask the designer or installer to account for heat, extraction and fire-safe materials.

### 8. Adding a focal point

Even a simple pergola can give height and structure to a flat garden. It can frame a view, mark the end of a path or connect planting beds with a patio.

The best-looking structure usually responds to its surroundings. Timber tends to sit naturally in traditional or heavily planted gardens, while slim powder-coated aluminium often suits contemporary paving and extensions. ## Does a pergola provide shade and rain protection?

All pergolas define a space, but their roofs perform differently:

- **Open beams or rafters** provide limited, moving shade and no meaningful rain cover.
- **Climbing plants** create natural dappled shade but take time to establish and are not waterproof.
- **Retractable fabric canopies** offer adjustable shade and light shower protection, subject to the fabric specification.
- **Fixed roofs** provide consistent cover but cannot be opened for direct sun or extra ventilation.
- **Louvred roofs** rotate between an open position and a closed, drained canopy, giving the most control over sun and rain.

This difference matters when deciding whether the pergola is mainly decorative or needs to keep a space usable in changeable weather. Compare the main options in our guide to [pergola roofs](/pergola-roofs/).

## Attached or freestanding: which suits the purpose?

An attached pergola works well when you want to extend daily living onto a patio directly outside the house. It keeps the outdoor area close to the kitchen and is convenient for dining, but fixing, drainage and the relationship with doors and windows must be designed carefully.

A freestanding pergola can go where the garden gets the best sun, view or privacy. It is often the better choice for a separate seating area, hot tub or focal point, although it needs its own foundations and may require a longer electrical run.

## What a pergola does not automatically do

A pergola is not necessarily waterproof, private or suitable for year-round use. Those benefits depend on its roof, sides, position and specification.

It is also different from a fully enclosed garden room. Adding extensive glazing, services or solid walls changes how the space behaves and may affect the design, ventilation, budget and permissions. Before ordering, check the current [pergola planning-permission guidance](/planning-permission/) for your property and proposed position.

## Is a pergola worthwhile for your garden?

A pergola is most worthwhile when it solves a specific problem. Decide whether you primarily need shade, rain cover, privacy, planting support or a clearly defined place to sit. That purpose should guide the roof type, material and location.

If you only want a frame for climbing plants, a straightforward timber pergola may be enough. If you expect to dine outside despite showers or want an outdoor room used across more of the year, a drained roof, side protection and professional foundations become more important.
