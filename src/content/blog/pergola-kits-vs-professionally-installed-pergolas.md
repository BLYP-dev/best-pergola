A pergola kit is usually the better choice when you want a standard-size structure, already have a suitable base and are confident assembling and anchoring it. Professional installation costs more, but it is better suited to made-to-measure designs, difficult sites and pergolas with louvred roofs, motors, drainage, screens or electrical work.

The choice is not simply “cheap DIY” versus “expensive professional”. Some kits are substantial aluminium systems, and some installation services only assemble a product on a prepared patio. Compare the complete scope—product, foundations, fixing, drainage, electrics and handover—before deciding which offers better value.

## Pergola kit or professional installation: quick comparison

| Factor | Pergola kit | Professionally installed pergola |
|---|---|---|
| Initial price | Usually lower | Higher because labour and project coordination are included |
| Sizes | Mostly standard modules | Standard or made to measure |
| Foundations | Normally your responsibility | Can be surveyed, specified and included |
| Assembly | You provide tools, labour and lifting help | Installation team erects and aligns the structure |
| Roof complexity | Best for open, fixed or simpler manual systems | Better for louvred, motorised and integrated systems |
| Drainage | Must be planned and connected by you | Can be coordinated with the structure and site |
| Electrics | Requires a competent electrician where applicable | Usually arranged or clearly assigned |
| Warranty | Depends on correct self-installation | Workmanship and product cover may be separate |
| Site risk | You assess access, levels and fixing points | Survey should identify constraints before ordering |
| Best for | Capable DIY buyers with a straightforward site | Complex, bespoke or higher-value projects |

## What is included in a pergola kit?

A kit usually contains the main structural components, roof parts where applicable, brackets and standard fixings. Components may be pre-cut or pre-drilled, with an installation manual showing the intended sequence.

Do not assume that “complete kit” means complete garden project. Depending on the seller and product, you may still need:

- Timber or other materials not supplied with a bracket kit
- Concrete pads, ground screws or base alterations
- Structural anchors suited to the actual substrate
- Sealants, flashing or rainwater fittings
- Electrical cable, isolation and connection
- Specialist drill bits or lifting equipment
- Extra people to move and support long components
- Disposal of packaging and excavated material

Check the component list before comparing prices. A kit that includes only the frame is not equivalent to one with a roof, gutters, blinds and anchoring hardware.

## What should professional installation include?

Professional installation can mean anything from assembly on a prepared patio to a managed design-and-build service. A full [pergola installation](/pergola-installation/) should ideally define:

1. Site survey and final dimensions
2. Foundation or fixing design
3. Delivery and access arrangements
4. Assembly, alignment and structural anchoring
5. Roof drainage and rainwater discharge
6. Electrical responsibilities for motors, lighting or heating
7. Testing and commissioning
8. Cleaning, waste removal and handover
9. Product and workmanship warranty terms

Ask for these items in writing. An inexpensive assembly service may be perfectly adequate for a straightforward kit, but it should not be compared with a quotation that includes excavation, foundations, electrical coordination and made-to-measure design.

## How much money can a pergola kit save?

Basic timber kits can begin in the hundreds of pounds, while larger aluminium roof systems run into several thousand. Professional fitting adds labour, but the apparent saving depends on how much site work you must arrange separately.

Budget for the finished result:

| Cost item | Kit route | Professional route |
|---|---|---|
| Pergola structure | Product price | Included in supplied-and-fitted quote |
| Delivery | Free, fixed fee or postcode-based | Often included, but confirm |
| Foundations | Separate materials or contractor | May be included or itemised |
| Assembly labour | Your time and helpers | Included |
| Specialist tools | Buy, hire or borrow | Installer provides |
| Electrics | Separate electrician | Included or coordinated |
| Remedial work | Your responsibility | Depends on contract and workmanship cover |

A kit offers the clearest saving when the base is ready, access is easy and the structure is genuinely designed for self-assembly. If you need a landscaper, electrician and separate assembly team, the price gap can narrow quickly.

## Foundations are the deciding factor more often than the frame

Pergola posts need to transfer wind and structural loads into a suitable base. The attractive surface you can see does not always provide that support.

### Installing on a patio

Sound concrete may accept the specified anchors. Paving laid on a shallow bed, loose slabs or porcelain tiles may need lifting so the structure can connect to concrete below. Drilling directly through the finished surface without understanding the substrate can crack slabs or create an unreliable fixing.

### Installing on decking

Decking boards alone are not structural foundations. Posts normally need reinforced joists, blocking or independent pads beneath the deck. Plan the fixing points before ordering because the frame dimensions determine where that support is needed.

### Installing on soil or lawn

A freestanding pergola on soft ground usually needs concrete pads, ground screws, piles or another designed foundation. Hole dimensions and fixing methods depend on the structure, ground and exposure; generic internet dimensions are not a substitute for the manufacturer's requirements or site-specific advice.

If the base is uncertain, a survey has more value than a lower product price.

## Which pergolas are realistic DIY projects?

DIY suitability depends on the product instructions and your skills, not just the material.

### Usually more DIY-friendly

- Small, open-roof timber pergolas
- Pre-cut freestanding structures on a prepared base
- Standard modules with clear instructions and accessible fixings
- Simple canopies without mains power or concealed drainage

### Usually better professionally installed

- Large or tall structures with heavy components
- Pergolas fixed to the house
- Multi-bay or made-to-measure designs
- Louvre roofs that depend on precise levelling
- Motorised roofs, screens, lighting or heating
- Structures with integrated gutters and concealed downpipes
- Sites with slopes, weak paving, decking or restricted access
- Exposed gardens where anchoring and wind loading need particular care

An aluminium pergola is not automatically unsuitable for DIY. Several UK suppliers sell self-assembly aluminium systems. The question is whether the specific model, weight, fixing method and site are manageable without compromising safety or performance.

## How long does each route take?

A stock kit can arrive sooner than a made-to-measure pergola, but assembly time begins only after the base, tools and helpers are ready. A simple kit may be erected within a day; larger structures can take a full weekend or longer, especially if instructions are unfamiliar.

A professional installation often has a longer pre-site phase. Survey, design approval, manufacture and scheduling may take several weeks, while the physical installation commonly takes one to five days. Our guide to [how long pergola installation takes](/how-long-does-pergola-installation-take/) separates lead time from on-site work.

Do not choose DIY solely to meet an event deadline. Delayed delivery, missing components, unsuitable foundations or poor weather can affect either route.

## Customisation and appearance

Kits are efficient because they use repeatable modules, standard colours and established spans. That can be ideal for a straightforward rectangular patio.

Professional and made-to-measure systems offer more flexibility where you need:

- Exact alignment with doors or architectural features
- Unusual width, projection or post positions
- A wall-mounted or wraparound layout
- Coordinated screens, glazing, lighting and heating
- A specific drainage outlet or concealed electrical route
- Non-standard colours or finishes

Customisation is only valuable when it solves a real constraint. A standard kit that fits the space cleanly may be better value than a bespoke structure with no practical advantage.

## Warranty and responsibility

Read the warranty before installation, not after a problem appears. Product warranties may require specified anchors, correct assembly, routine maintenance and approved electrical work. Keep the instructions, order details and photographs of concealed foundations or cable routes.

With professional installation, clarify who is responsible for the structure, finish, motors and workmanship. These may have different warranty periods and different contacts. Also ask whether the installer or manufacturer handles replacement parts and adjustments.

Professional fitting does not remove the need to read the contract. Self-assembly does not automatically invalidate a warranty. The written terms for the exact product decide.

## Safety and permissions

Large beams, aluminium roof sections and glass panels can be awkward or dangerous to lift. Follow the manufacturer's stated team size, tools and sequence. Mains electrical work should be completed by someone competent for that work.

The route you choose does not change planning rules. A kit pergola can still be too tall, too close to a boundary or unsuitable for a listed property. Check the current [pergola planning-permission guidance](/planning-permission/) before ordering a structure that may be difficult to return.

## Which option should you choose?

Choose a pergola kit when:

- The design and size suit your garden without alteration
- The site is accessible and the base is ready
- You understand the anchoring and drainage requirements
- You have the tools, time and lifting help
- Any electrical work can be arranged safely
- The potential saving justifies taking responsibility for the build

Choose professional installation when:

- The structure is expensive, motorised or made to measure
- The foundations or existing surface are uncertain
- The pergola attaches to the house
- You need coordinated drainage, screens, lighting or heating
- Access, levels or exposure complicate the work
- You want one written specification covering supply and fitting
